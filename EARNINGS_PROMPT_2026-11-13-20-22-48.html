Corporate earnings outlook: US and European equities

Introduction

Information cutoff: November 13, 2026

Scope: S&P 500 and STOXX Europe 600 aggregates, supplemented by company disclosures. Company examples illustrate business mechanisms—not market-wide trends. “Blended” figures combine reported results with estimates for companies yet to report.

1. Executive findings

  • Aggregate profit growth remains strong in both regions, but composition matters. The latest verified US snapshot shows substantial growth even excluding energy. Europe’s headline growth is considerably more dependent on energy; its non-energy earnings outlook is improving, but much less dramatically. (lipperalpha.refinitiv.com)
  • US momentum is strongest in energy and technology; communication services and materials have suffered adverse estimate-to-outturn movements. Positive year-over-year growth should not be confused with improving expectations. (advantage.factset.com)
  • Margins and earnings quality are uneven. AI infrastructure supports supplier revenues but absorbs customers’ cash; commodity inflation prompts price increases; automotive competition and currency movements pressure profitability. Reported earnings growth can also reflect changes in investment gains, restructuring charges and other adjustments rather than stronger operations. (microsoft.com)

4. Business implications: reported results versus management guidance

Microsoft: strong operations, but capital intensity and accounting comparisons matter

Published October 28; fiscal Q1 2027 ended September 30, 2026.

  • Reported: revenue increased 19% to $92.3 billion, operating income 21%, and GAAP EPS 32%. EPS growth excluding investment effects was 18%; prior-year investment losses materially affected the comparison.
  • Margins/cash: operating margin was approximately 50%, but cloud gross margin declined to 66%, reflecting mix toward Azure. Operating cash flow reached $58 billion, up 29%; cash property-and-equipment spending of $39.4 billion left free cash flow of $18.6 billion.
  • Management guidance, fiscal Q2 2027: operating margin expected to decline slightly YoY; FX expected to have no meaningful growth impact.
  • Transmission: demand supports earnings, but infrastructure spending limits immediately distributable cash and creates a larger future depreciation base. [S5–S6] (microsoft.com)

Cisco: guidance raised despite gross-margin compression

Published November 12; fiscal Q1 2027 ended October 24, 2026.

  • Reported: revenue $18.5 billion, +24%; non-GAAP EPS +40%. Non-GAAP gross margin fell to 65.6% from 68.1%, while operating margin reached a record 37.8%. Operating cash flow increased 36% to $4.4 billion.
  • Management guidance, fiscal 2027: raised revenue outlook to $74.8–75.8 billion and non-GAAP EPS to $5.47–5.53. Expected hyperscaler AI-infrastructure revenue increased to $9 billion. The previous numerical guidance range was not independently verified here.
  • Transmission: strong networking sales and expense leverage can lift earnings despite lower gross margins; revenue growth should not be read as pricing-power evidence alone. [S7] (s21.q4cdn.com)

Shell: strong cash generation, with material timing and non-operating effects

Published October 29; Q3 2026.

  • Reported: adjusted earnings $10.814 billion, versus $5.432 billion a year earlier; operating cash flow $21.043 billion, versus $12.207 billion; company-defined free cash flow $16.322 billion.
  • Higher refining margins and realised LNG prices supported sequential earnings. Reported profit also included $0.8 billion of net identified gains, while operating cash flow benefited from $3.4 billion of derivative-related inflows.
  • Transmission: stronger cash generation supports deleveraging and distributions, but derivative settlements, working-capital timing and commodity conditions limit extrapolation of one quarter’s conversion rate. [S8] (shell.gcs-web.com)

Mercedes-Benz: reported recovery masks weaker underlying profitability

Published October 28; Q3 2026.

  • Reported: revenue declined 4.5%; EBIT increased 136.4%, largely because adjustments were lower. Adjusted EBIT fell 15%.
  • Cars’ adjusted return on sales declined to 2.5% from 4.8%. Currency headwinds and intensifying Chinese competition weighed on results.
  • Industrial free cash flow fell 87.4% to €172 million, reflecting a temporary new-vehicle inventory build.
  • Transmission: competition and currency pressure reduce operating profit; inventories absorb cash before sales convert into receipts. Better statutory EBIT therefore does not establish an underlying recovery. [S9] (media.mercedes-benz.com)

Unilever: volume growth offsets part of commodity pressure

Publication date: null; observation Q3 2026.

  • Reported: underlying sales growth 5.2%, comprising volume 2.9% and price 2.3%; pricing responded to higher commodity costs. Developed-market retail Foods remained soft.
  • Management guidance, calendar 2026: upgraded outlook to underlying sales growth 4–6%, at least 3% volume growth, and modest underlying operating-margin improvement from 20.0% in 2025.
  • Transmission: volume supports fixed-cost absorption, while pricing protects margins against inputs. This company evidence does not demonstrate staples-wide demand strength. [S10] (unilever.com)

Novo Nordisk: an upgrade can still imply approximately flat annual earnings

Publication date: null; guidance comparison dated November 4 versus August 4, 2026.

  • Management guidance, calendar 2026: adjusted sales and operating-profit growth raised to −2% to +2% at constant exchange rates, from −6% to 0%, reflecting stronger GLP-1 sales expectations.
  • Transmission: the upgrade improves expected revenue and earnings, but does not imply strong full-year growth or establish healthcare-sector-wide improvement. [S11] (novonordisk.com)

5. Contradictory evidence and currency effects

  • Broad beats, limited aggregate upside: FactSet’s November 6 US snapshot showed 84% beating EPS estimates, but aggregate earnings only 2.7% above expectations. Beat frequency overstated the size of the positive surprise. [S3] (advantage.factset.com)
  • Currency is company-specific: Microsoft cloud growth was 30% reported versus 31% constant currency, indicating a modest translation drag. Mercedes reported currency headwinds without a quantified bridge in the evidence reviewed. Novo’s revised 2026 outlook expected Danish-krone growth in line with constant-currency growth, replacing earlier FX drags. These disclosures do not establish one uniform multinational FX effect. (microsoft.com)
  • Valuation interpretation: stronger recurring earnings support the earnings denominator of valuation multiples; investment gains, reduced restructuring charges or temporarily favourable cash settlements deserve different treatment. Microsoft, Mercedes and Shell demonstrate why earnings-to-cash reconciliation matters alongside headline EPS. This is an analytical interpretation, not a valuation forecast. (microsoft.com)

6. Missing data and verification limitations

  • The latest verified US aggregate was November 12; the forward-consensus and margin tables are explicitly November 6, not November 13.
  • European figures use Reuters’ reproduction of LSEG data. Detailed latest European sector tables, calendar-2026/2027 consensus and revision breadth were not independently verified.
  • No comparable market-wide cash-flow, accrual-quality or one-off-adjusted earnings series was verified. Company cash-flow examples cannot establish aggregate earnings quality.
  • Broad labour, tariff, financing-cost and hedging bridges were not verified. Absence of that evidence is not evidence those pressures are absent.
  • Provider differences and changing reporting samples prevent treating differences between LSEG and FactSet snapshots as clean revisions.
