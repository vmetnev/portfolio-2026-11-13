U.S. Equity Markets — Evidence Report

Introduction

Information cutoff: November 13, 2026

Scope assumption: U.S. equities, with the S&P 500 as the large-cap reference. November 13 closing prices were not verified; intraday observations are identified separately.

1. Executive findings

  • Demand and earnings evidence remains supportive, but not uniformly so. Third-quarter domestic demand strengthened, while the verified November 6 earnings snapshot showed improving S&P 500 earnings growth. These observations support revenues and earnings, but do not establish that subsequent quarters will sustain the same pace. (bea.gov)
  • Inflation remains a constraint on valuation expansion. October headline inflation slowed month over month, but underlying consumer and producer measures remained persistent. The Federal Reserve held rates unchanged in October, with two voters preferring an increase—not a cut. (bls.gov)
  • Index weakness has not meant uniform business deterioration. October returns favored equal-weight equities over capitalization-weighted equities, while bank lending evidence showed greater pressure on small firms. Strong cloud growth also coexists with substantial infrastructure spending and cash-flow demands. (cboe.com)

3. Changes in direction or momentum

  • Activity improved: Q3 growth accelerated, and October payroll growth strengthened relative to September’s revised reading. These are backward-looking improvements, not Q4 forecasts. (bea.gov)
  • Headline inflation cooled; underlying inflation did not clearly break lower: monthly CPI and PPI slowed, while core CPI and PPI excluding food, energy and trade services maintained their preceding monthly rates. (bls.gov)
  • Earnings expectations improved while leadership rotated: the verified earnings snapshot strengthened relative to September estimates, but October capitalization-weighted returns lagged equal-weight returns substantially. (advantage.factset.com)

4. Implications for businesses and equity markets

Analytical interpretation: The evidence separates three channels: stronger demand supporting revenues; persistent inflation and financing costs threatening margins; and lower multiples offering less demanding pricing only if forecast earnings materialize. These channels can offset one another, so earnings growth need not translate directly into comparable share-price gains. (bea.gov)

Capital-intensive cloud businesses and smaller bank-dependent firms face different constraints: the former must convert infrastructure spending into profitable utilization, while the latter face tighter credit access. Neither exposure is adequately represented by aggregate index earnings alone. (microsoft.com)

5. Contradictory evidence

  • Strong employment versus underemployment: despite payroll growth, involuntary part-time employment increased 339,000, and U-6 underutilization rose from 7.6% to 7.9%. Consumer-income resilience is therefore uneven. (bls.gov)
  • Annual margin improvement versus sequential deterioration: FactSet’s blended Q3 net margin was 14.9%, above 13.0% a year earlier but below 17.0% in Q2. Earnings strength does not demonstrate uninterrupted margin expansion. (advantage.factset.com)
  • Quarterly disinflation versus persistent monthly readings: Q3 core PCE inflation slowed to 2.5% annualized from 3.3%, while October core CPI remained persistent. These measures cover different baskets and observation windows; they should not be treated as interchangeable. (bea.gov)
